# INSY 674 machine-learning report

## 1. Frame the problem and look at the big picture

The existing project is an F1-style simulator for students and researchers comparing driving systems under repeatable conditions. The focused real-data question here is: **Can historical telemetry and track-relative features predict a driver's throttle and braking behavior?**

Throttle is a continuous supervised-regression target on `[0, 1]`. Brake is a binary classification target (`0` off, `1` on); it is not brake pressure. Inputs include speed, lateral offset, heading error and current/future track curvature. Lap identity is only a split group. Offline prediction asks how well the recorded controls are imitated; closed-loop simulator behavior is a separate system-level question.

This is offline learning from a fixed historical dataset, not online adaptation, reinforcement learning or real-car autonomy. The telemetry is appropriate for a narrow pilot because it contains real throttle/brake labels and road-relative signals, but not for broad driver/circuit generalization.

## 2. Explore the data

The saved FastF1 export contains **1,774 rows across three laps (590, 591 and 593 rows), one driver, one qualifying session and one circuit**. Training development uses laps 5 and 11 (1,181 rows); lap 14 (593 rows) was repeatedly inspected and is exploratory only. The six raw fields are lap-relative time (s), source-frame x/y (m), speed (m/s), throttle (0–1) and brake (binary).

The saved rows contain no missing values or duplicate records; timestamps are unique within each lap. The median merged sampling interval is about **0.133 s**, while the native source metadata permits gaps up to 1.3 s and documents channel interpolation. Consequently, zero missing cells do not mean every signal value is a native sensor observation. Across the three laps, **231/1,774 brake labels are positive (13.0%)**, so accuracy alone is misleading. Mean speeds are about 72.6–73.3 m/s, showing the absence of standing-start and low-speed recovery examples.

Reproducible profiles, interval/outlier diagnostics, correlations, lap comparisons and labeled figures are generated by [ml_analysis/eda.py](../ml_analysis/eda.py). Plot interpretations are saved in `output/rubric_analysis/eda_interpretations.md`. IQR flags are reported, not automatically removed: extreme telemetry may describe a genuine racing state. Existing track-relative records show per-lap lateral-offset and speed/control behavior, but the alignment and interpolation add measurement uncertainty.

## 3. Prepare the data

The initial source data are saved CSV exports; no fresh download is required. Existing `run_pipeline.py` converts positions to track-relative coordinates and engineering features use the simulator's shared track geometry. The supplemental CV starts from raw telemetry, estimates the coordinate transform from the training lap only in each fold, applies that transform to the validation lap, then derives features. The transform uses training-lap positions, not held-out targets.

Laps are the independent groups. With two training laps, two-fold `GroupKFold` is the maximum meaningful N-fold split. Imputation and scaling, where required, are encapsulated in scikit-learn Pipelines and fit inside each training fold. Tree models do not require scaling. Throttle predictions are clipped to the physical target range; brake predictions remain 0/1.

No observations were removed solely for being extreme. The source stream is irregular and may be interpolated across gaps; the saved records should not be treated as equally spaced independent samples. The original shared alignment export used lap 5 for all saved aligned laps; the new fold-aware evaluation avoids that particular calibration leakage.

## 4. Shortlist promising models

Six regression and six classification candidates are compared. The objective requires different model tasks: continuous throttle regression and binary brake classification. The raw group count is too small to support a strong claim that any candidate will generalize to other sessions.

The new two-fold training-only alignment CV selected **HistGradientBoosting for throttle** (MAE 0.0394 ± 0.0001; RMSE 0.0943 ± 0.0032; R² 0.9364 ± 0.0050) among the screened candidates. Random Forest (MAE 0.0553 ± 0.0029) and SVR (0.0564 ± 0.0024) remain useful complementary baselines and already have simulator artifacts. For brake classification, Random Forest had the strongest balanced accuracy (0.9429 ± 0.0225) and F1 (0.8660 ± 0.0226). Gradient Boosting (F1 0.8437 ± 0.0163) and SVC (F1 0.8082 ± 0.0081) are alternatives. The always-off Dummy classifier has F1 0 and balanced accuracy 0.5.

The summary figures are generated as the bar/error-bar panels in `output/rubric_analysis/figures/model_comparison.png`. Feature importance is descriptive; curvature channels are correlated, so impurity importance is not causal evidence.

## 6. Fine-tune the system

A seeded six-candidate randomized search per target used the same two lap groups and fold-specific alignment. Tuning HistGradientBoosting reduced throttle MAE from **0.0394 ± 0.0001** to **0.0377 ± 0.0002**. The tuned Random Forest brake classifier achieved F1 **0.8570 ± 0.0276**, below its untuned value of **0.8660 ± 0.0226**. Thus, tuning gave a small throttle improvement but did not improve brake classification. Search candidates, fit durations and fold scores are recorded in `hyperparameter_trials.csv`. No test set was used for tuning.

These settings are not declared final models: with just two groups, any apparent ranking is unstable. See `hyperparameter_trials.csv` for candidate parameters, fold scores and elapsed time.

## 7. Present the solution: ensembles and simulator evidence

On identical grouped folds, equal RF/SVR voting reduced throttle MAE to **0.0533 ± 0.0005**, compared with RF 0.0553 ± 0.0029 and SVR 0.0564 ± 0.0024. This modest difference is exploratory. The soft RF/logistic brake ensemble had F1 **0.7550 ± 0.0541**, below the standalone RF (0.8660 ± 0.0226); it is not recommended as an improvement. Stacking was omitted because two independent training groups do not support leakage-safe meta-training.

The preserved Spa benchmark is a frozen **simulator driving** evaluation, not offline real-telemetry prediction. Planner-only completes in 287.4 s; tuned RF + planner in 282.0 s; tuned SVR + planner 281.9 s; tuned neural pedal + planner 276.8 s; RF/SVR blend + planner 281.2 s; frozen full-action driver 294.3 s. Each has zero off-track departures and a valid lap under the saved benchmark. These are deterministic system comparisons with differing control/model details, not a pure ranking of telemetry prediction algorithms. The full-action policy imitates a simulated expert; it was not trained on real steering labels.

## 8. Assumptions and limitations

- Two independent training laps from one driver/session/circuit are insufficient for broad generalization claims.
- Lap 14 has been repeatedly inspected and cannot serve as an untouched test.
- There is no independent real-telemetry prediction test in the available data. The preserved Spa test evaluates a simulated controller only.
- Saved telemetry has irregular sampling and FastF1 interpolation across native streams; source gaps can reach the documented 1.3-second policy.
- Position alignment, track geometry and track-relative features are imperfect; the prior saved EDA tables use the historical lap-5 shared transform.
- Brake is an occurrence label, not pressure; no measured steering target exists.
- Qualifying-speed observations omit standing-start and substantial recovery behavior.
- Results do not establish real-car performance. Simulator physics, road boundaries and controller assistance differ from real F1.

What worked: grouped validation reduced row leakage; Random Forest brake classification substantially beat the always-off baseline; the tree boosting regression was promising for throttle. What failed: the MLP brake classifier nearly ignored the positive class, and the soft classification ensemble degraded F1. What surprised us: high overall brake accuracy can coexist with poor positive-class recall, while braking-zone throttle errors are several times straight-segment errors.

## 10. Future improvements

Collect new drivers, sessions, circuits and low-speed/recovery examples; reserve an entirely untouched telemetry session before any model decisions; retain native timestamps and interpolation provenance; validate alignment/calibration inside every fold; add repeated simulator conditions; and evaluate practical brake cost asymmetry before choosing a classifier threshold. Only after independent telemetry data supports it should final offline test metrics be reported.
